Snoo vs Cradlewise: Which Smart Bassinet Should You Buy?

Not medical advice. For emergencies, call your local emergency number.

If you are shopping for a smart bassinet, two names come up more than any others: the Snoo by Happiest Baby and the Cradlewise. Both promise more sleep for the whole family, both cost serious money, and both have fiercely loyal parents behind them. But they solve the problem in very different ways, and the right choice depends on what you actually need. Here is an honest, feature-by-feature comparison.

The short version

The Snoo is the better pure newborn soothing machine, with a rental option and the strongest safety story. The Cradlewise is the better long-term value: one purchase that works from birth to age two, with a built-in monitor. If you only want help surviving the first six months, rent or buy the Snoo. If you want to buy once and be done, get the Cradlewise.

What the Snoo is

The Snoo Smart Sleeper is made by Happiest Baby, the company founded by pediatrician Dr. Harvey Karp. It is a bassinet for babies from birth until they can roll over, usually around five to six months, or 25 pounds. When it hears fussing, it responds on its own: gentle rocking and white noise that automatically escalate through several levels until the baby settles.

The signature feature is the Snoo Sack, a swaddle that clips into the sides of the bassinet so the baby stays on their back all night. It is the first and only bassinet to receive FDA De Novo authorization for keeping sleeping infants on their backs, which is the single most important safe-sleep position.

Price: the Snoo sells new for about $1,695. Certified pre-loved (refurbished) units run roughly $1,195 to $1,395 depending on sales, and rentals start around $149 to $159 per month. One thing to know before you buy: full app functionality now sits behind a Premium subscription. A new purchase includes nine months free; after that it is $19.99 per month. That change, introduced in 2024, annoyed a lot of existing owners, so factor it into the real cost.

What the Cradlewise is

Cradlewise takes a different approach. It is a smart bassinet that converts into a full-size crib, designed to last from birth to 24 months (up to 33 pounds). Instead of reacting to full crying, its AI watches for early wake cues and starts a gentle bounce before the baby fully wakes up.

It has a built-in video monitor with night vision, plays white, pink, and brown noise plus streamed music, and tracks sleep in the companion app. The Nurture Core plan that powers the app features is free for two years, and no paid subscription is required to use the crib itself. It ships with a VOC-free mattress, a waterproof cover, and fitted sheets.

Price: Cradlewise sells for $1,799, often shown against a $1,999 list price. There is no rental program, so you are committing to the full purchase.

Choose the Snoo if...

  • You want maximum help specifically during the newborn months, when sleep deprivation hits hardest.
  • You like the rental option: try it for a month or two before committing to a purchase.
  • Back-sleeping enforcement matters to you and you want the FDA-authorized design.
  • You plan to resell afterward. Snoos have the deepest secondhand market of any baby product in this category, which brings the real six-month cost well below sticker.

Choose the Cradlewise if...

  • You want one purchase to cover birth through age two, not a bassinet now and a crib later.
  • You want a built-in monitor and would rather not buy and mount a separate one.
  • You prefer soothing that starts before the crying starts rather than escalating after it.
  • You do not want another subscription in your life.

Honest caveats

Two things worth knowing before spending this much. First, Happiest Baby's own data says about one in five babies never really take to the Snoo. If you are unsure, that is exactly what the rental is for: try before you buy. Second, the Cradlewise is a big, heavy piece of furniture at around 88 pounds. It is not moving between rooms, and some owners report the AI occasionally soothing an empty crib.

And the most honest caveat of all: neither product is necessary. Plenty of well-rested families used a basic bassinet, an inexpensive white noise machine, and a swaddle. What you are buying here is automation and, on the hardest nights, your sanity.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use the Snoo past 6 months?

No. Stop when your baby shows signs of rolling, reaches 25 pounds, or is around five to six months old, whichever comes first. The app includes a weaning mode that gradually reduces motion to ease the transition to a regular crib.

Does the Cradlewise need WiFi or a paid plan to work?

The app features need WiFi, and the free Nurture Core plan covers the first two years. But the crib itself does not require a paid subscription to function day to day.

Which one actually helps babies sleep more?

Both companies claim meaningful extra sleep and parent reviews support that both help. The honest answer depends on the baby: babies who calm with motion tend to love the Snoo, while light sleepers who stir often may do better with Cradlewise catching wake-ups early.

Which holds its resale value better?

The Snoo, by a wide margin. Its secondhand market is deep and active, so buying new and reselling after six months can bring the effective cost close to the rental price.
