The 4-Month Sleep Regression: What's Happening and How to Help

The 4-month sleep regression is a permanent change in how your baby sleeps: light and deep sleep now cycle like adult sleep, so waking between cycles becomes normal. Expect more night waking and shorter naps for two to four weeks. Keep wake windows near 1.5 to 2 hours, protect the first nap, and ride it out.

Not medical advice: this page is general information for parents, not a diagnosis or treatment plan. If your baby is sick, feeding poorly, breathing oddly or you simply feel something is wrong, call your pediatrician. For emergencies, call your local emergency number.

Signs it is happening now

  • Waking every 1–2 hours at night when she used to stretch 4–5 hours
  • Naps collapsing to 30–45 minutes, often ending the moment you set her down
  • Fighting the fourth catnap or dropping it altogether
  • More alert, smiley and grabby awake — the 4-month developmental burst
  • Cluster feeding or wanting to eat more often than usual

How long does it last?

Count two to four weeks as typical, with the first 7–10 nights the hardest. If broken sleep goes past a month, or snoring, breathing pauses, fever or poor feeding come with it, ask your pediatrician instead of waiting it out.

What to do tonight

  1. Hold wake windows at 1.5–2 hours and wake your baby by 7:00 AM so the day has an anchor.
  2. Protect the first nap of the day (it is the deepest) even if it means a contact nap.
  3. Put your baby down drowsy but awake for at least one sleep a day to practice falling back asleep.
  4. Keep the 7:00–8:00 PM bedtime — an overtired 4-month-old wakes more, not less.
  5. Use one settled response per wake (patting, shushing, feeding on a schedule) so nights stay predictable.

Quick schedule reference for 4 months

Wake windows1.5–2 hours before each nap
Naps3–4 (two full naps + a 45-minute catnap)
Night sleep10–12 hours, often in 2–3 hour chunks
Bedtime7:00–8:00 PM

Typical ranges for healthy children, not targets. Tune your own times with the wake window calculator or the month-by-month schedules (13–36 months).

What the guidelines say

The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) recommends a consistent bedtime routine and a separate, flat sleep surface for infants. The CDC puts total sleep at 12–16 hours a day for babies 4–12 months, 11–14 hours for ages 1–2 and 10–13 hours for ages 3–5, and the National Sleep Foundation (NSF) ranges match those numbers. Use them as ranges, not targets: total sleep differs from child to child.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 4-month sleep regression permanent?
The change in sleep cycles is permanent, but the chaos is not. Most families are back to their usual pattern within two to four weeks once everyone adapts to the new light-and-deep cycle.
Should I feed my baby every time she wakes at 4 months?
At 4 months the AAP still expects night feeds, but a waking is not always hunger. Try a brief wait or a settle first, and keep feeds at a steady rhythm so waking does not become a feeding habit.
Will sleep training survive the 4-month regression?
If you had started, pause for the worst week and keep the bedtime routine identical, then resume. Consistency at bedtime matters more than perfect nights mid-regression.
When should I call the pediatrician?
Call if waking comes with fever, vomiting, breathing trouble, poor weight gain or you are at breaking point. A tired parent deserves support too — ask about safe sleep and shifts with a partner.
