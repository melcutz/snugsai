    Every child is different — these are typical ranges, not targets. Watch sleepy cues and shift times by 15–30 minutes to fit your family.

    Typical nap transitions at a glance

    Transition Typical age Signs it's near Total sleep (24 h)
    3 naps → 2 naps 5–8 months The third (cat)nap disappears on its own; the middle wake window stretches 12–15 h
    2 naps → 1 nap 8–15 months Nap fighting, bedtime drift, short or skipped second nap 11–14 h
    1 nap → none 18–36 months Refusing the nap 3+ days a week, falling asleep at 6 p.m. 11–14 h, including quiet time

    Ranges follow typical guidance: the American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) and CDC recommend 12–16 hours of sleep for babies aged 4–12 months and 11–14 hours for toddlers aged 1–2 years, and the National Sleep Foundation (NSF) lists 10–13 hours for three-year-olds.

    Frequently asked questions

    When do babies drop to one nap?
    Most babies move from two naps to one between 8 and 15 months, with the change often landing around the first birthday. The American Academy of Pediatrics and CDC describe 11–14 hours of sleep for toddlers aged 1–2 years, which fits a single midday nap plus night sleep.
    How long does a nap transition take?
    Plan on one to two weeks. Shift nap time later by 15–30 minutes every two or three days, keep bedtime anchored, and expect a few short or skipped naps while the new rhythm settles.
    What happens if I drop a nap too early?
    An overtired child often fights sleep harder, wakes more at night, and melts down by dinner. If that happens, restore the old schedule for a few days or offer a short early-afternoon nap, then try again in one to two weeks.
    Is this medical advice?
    No. The checker shares typical ranges described by the AAP, CDC and National Sleep Foundation for planning purposes only. It is not medical advice — talk to your pediatrician about your child's sleep.
